# Cover Letter Structure

## Gate: ask first
Do not draft until the user has answered:
1. Organization and role title.
2. What specifically interests them about this organization or role.
3. Which experience or angle they want to lead with.

If the posting does not ask for a cover letter, tell the user it is often
optional and let them decide.

## Five-paragraph structure (about 300–450 words, one Letter-size page)

1. **Strategic framing.** A point of view on the role or function, not "I am
   applying for…". Prompt: *What do you believe about this kind of work that
   a hiring manager might not expect?*
2. **Point of view in practice.** The principle behind how the user works.
   Prompt: *Where has that principle changed an outcome?*
3. **Evidence.** Two or three concrete examples with scope and a verifiable
   result. Prompt: *Which achievements from your profile map most directly to
   the posting's top three requirements?*
4. **Why this organization and role.** Specific, researched, not
   interchangeable with any other employer. Prompt: *What did you learn about
   them that is not on the first page of their website?*
5. **Forward-looking close (short).** Confident, concise, no grovelling.
   Prompt: *What do you want to be doing in this role in a year?*

## Format rules
- Address a named person when the posting or research gives one; otherwise
  use "Dear Hiring Manager". Avoid "To whom it may concern".
- No generic openers and no restating the resume line by line.
- Mirror the posting's vocabulary only where the user has the experience.
- Leave out the same personal details as the resume (photo, date of birth,
  marital status, nationality, SIN).
- Warm but professional; active voice.
- Default coach output is an outline plus prompts. Provide full prose only if
  asked, and mark it as a draft for the user to rewrite.
